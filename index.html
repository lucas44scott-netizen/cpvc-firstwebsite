Bonk Prevention Science

Understanding the limits of human muscle glycogen and exogenous substrate absorption.

Why does bonking happen?

Your body stores roughly 1,800 to 2,000 calories of glycogen in the liver and skeletal muscles. During Zone 3 or higher efforts, muscle glycogen depletes rapidly within 90 to 120 minutes. Once glycogen reserves empty, carbohydrate oxidation plummets, causing extreme neurological fatigue, weak contractions, and severe pacing drop.

Why 90 grams of carbohydrate per hour?

Intestinal SGLT1 transporters absorb glucose at a hard limit of approximately 60 grams per hour. Adding fructose utilizes GLUT5 transporters, unlocking up to 90 to 120 grams per hour without triggering gastrointestinal distress or stomach distress.

How do short workouts vs ultra endurance compare?

For efforts under 45 to 60 minutes, your baseline glycogen stores are more than sufficient. For workouts lasting over 90 minutes, starting exogenous intake immediately in minute 15 is crucial because intestinal absorption takes 20 to 30 minutes to enter your bloodstream.
